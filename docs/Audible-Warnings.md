# Audible Warning System

## Purpose

Add an audible warning channel to supplement the gauge's visual warnings. The Waveshare ESP32-S3-Touch-LCD-5 has no onboard buzzer or speaker, so the design uses an external marine-suitable active piezo buzzer.

The custom gauge remains supplemental. Its alarm behavior must not replace or silence the Yamaha or Garmin warning systems, and engine-warning mappings must be validated on the boat before they are treated as authoritative.

## Hardware Direction

Use a waterproof or panel-sealed active piezo buzzer with these target characteristics:

- 5-12 V DC operating range compatible with the selected supply
- self-oscillating active buzzer; firmware only switches it on and off
- less than 100 mA operating current, leaving substantial margin below the board output rating
- loud enough to hear at the helm, with final loudness confirmed during an underway test
- gasketed panel mounting or protected mounting behind a purpose-designed acoustic opening

The buzzer is controlled through Waveshare isolated digital output DO0. DO1 remains available as a spare.

## Electrical Design

The Waveshare digital outputs are 5-36 V open-drain sink outputs, rated up to 450 mA per channel. The buzzer must not be powered directly from an ESP32 GPIO.

Conceptual connection:

```text
Fused 5-12 V positive  ---- buzzer positive
Buzzer negative       ---- DO0 switched output
Supply/output return  ---- output common per Waveshare terminal diagram
```

Implementation rules:

- match the buzzer voltage rating to its supply
- use a dedicated low-current fuse near the supply connection
- target less than 100 mA load current
- confirm DO0 and output-common terminal labels against the exact board revision and schematic before energizing
- use marine-grade stranded wire, ferrules or suitable crimp terminals, strain relief, and corrosion protection
- add a flyback diode only if the selected sounder is inductive; a normal piezo sounder does not require one
- default the output to off during boot, reset, OTA, and fault recovery
- never connect the buzzer load to GPIO15 or GPIO16, which are reserved for CAN

## Warning Priorities and Patterns

| Priority | Meaning | Audible pattern | Visual behavior |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Feedback | Touch/button confirmation | One 75 ms chirp | Normal control feedback |
| Advisory | Noncritical condition | 250 ms on, 1750 ms off | Amber banner/icon |
| Warning | Prompt operator attention | 250 ms on, 250 ms off, three pulses; repeat every 3 seconds | Red warning banner |
| Critical | Immediate attention | Continuous tone, with a 100 ms gap every 2 seconds to prove the output is actively controlled | Full red critical overlay |

Patterns are generated by a non-blocking state machine. No alarm routine may delay CAN reception, UI refresh, logging, or the watchdog.

A higher-priority alarm preempts a lower-priority pattern. When multiple conditions share a priority, the display lists all active conditions while the buzzer plays only the highest active pattern.

## Candidate Alarm Sources

Initial candidates include:

- validated NMEA engine warning/status bits
- high engine temperature
- low system/alternator voltage
- high RPM or RPM outside a configured safe range
- loss or staleness of required engine data while the engine appears to be running
- internal gauge faults such as persistent CAN or sensor-data loss

Exact thresholds are configuration values, not assumptions embedded in UI code. Default thresholds must remain disabled or clearly marked provisional until confirmed against Yamaha documentation and observed boat data.

## Acknowledge, Silence, and Recovery

All detected alarm conditions and their lifecycle transitions shall be recorded
persistently and available in an on-screen history view, regardless of audible
enablement or priority preemption. See [Alarm Logging and History](Alarm-History.md)
for occurrence identity, timestamps, recovery, export and retention.

- Touching the visible warning opens an alarm detail panel.
- The operator may silence the current audible alarm for a configurable period.
- Silencing does not clear the visual warning or the underlying condition.
- A new higher-priority alarm cancels the silence and sounds immediately.
- A cleared condition stops its pattern and remains in recent alarm history.
- If an acknowledged condition clears and later returns, it sounds again.
- Critical alarms may be acknowledged but should not remain permanently muted.
- Touch-feedback chirps are independently configurable and are suppressed whenever an advisory, warning, or critical alarm is active.

## Settings and Diagnostics

Settings should include:

- master audible alarms enabled/disabled
- touch-feedback chirp enabled/disabled
- normal and night quiet-mode behavior
- silence duration
- provisional threshold values
- dockside buzzer test for each pattern

Diagnostics should show:

- DO0 commanded state
- active alarm priority and pattern phase
- current and recently cleared alarm conditions
- acknowledgement/silence state and remaining time
- alarm source, source address, PGN, and data age when applicable

Settings persist across restart and OTA. Safety-relevant alarm enablement should not be silently changed during firmware updates.

## Validation Plan

Before relying on audible warnings:

1. Verify DO0 remains off during power-up, reset, and firmware upload.
2. Measure buzzer supply voltage and operating current.
3. Run every pattern dockside and confirm the silence/acknowledge rules.
4. Confirm the sound is audible at the helm with the engine operating.
5. Simulate stale data and verify that CAN processing and UI refresh continue normally.
6. Compare every engine-warning indication against the Garmin/Yamaha displays.
7. Record validated PGNs, status bits, thresholds, and test results.
